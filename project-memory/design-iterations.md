# Design iterations

The project started as a Canva Code landing page and went through these rounds of feedback and refinement, in order.

## 1. First review of the Canva version
**Found:** duplicate page sections and IDs, a contact form that asked how to reply but had nowhere to enter a phone or email, flat type sizes, mismatched card labels, too many "coming soon" dead ends, and emergency info buried at the bottom.
**Changed:** removed the duplicates, added phone and email fields, set a real type scale (DM Serif Display with Work Sans), moved emergency info to a bar at the top, merged four repeated "find care" sections into one, and replaced "coming soon" text with believable sample content.

## 2. Richer content
**Added:** doctor cards, a patient quote, an illustrated campus map, a gentle scroll fade-in, the magnolia watermark, and the green card fill on hover.

## 3. Hospital image fixes
**Found:** the hospital image looked stretched, and its purple backing shape did not line up.
**Changed:** framed the image at its true square shape and made the purple backing an even border around it.

## 4. A more realistic map
**Found:** the map did not look like a real embedded map.
**Changed:** rebuilt it in the style of a web map, with a street grid, building outlines, parks, a pin, an address card with directions, and zoom, drag and keyboard controls.

## 5. Hospital essentials
**Added:** insurance and billing, live "Open now" hours, classes and events, an FAQ, footer links, language assistance, social icons, a favicon and link-preview tags.

## 6. Formal design review
**Found:** muted gray text just under AA contrast on tinted backgrounds, phone numbers breaking across lines, faint placeholders, and an ungrouped footer.
**Changed:** darkened the gray, kept phone numbers on one line, fixed the placeholders, grouped the footer into three labeled columns, and tightened the hours table.

## 7. Images not showing outside Canva
**Found:** the logo and hospital image used Canva-only links, and the scroll fade-in could leave sections invisible in some preview tools.
**Changed:** added local backup images and a safety net that shows all content if the fade-in cannot run.

## 8. Sources page and an emergency department
**Added:** a sources page with APA citations, and the Wellstar Cobb Medical Center page as source material.
**Decision:** a "Regional Medical Center" with hospital beds should have an emergency department, so one was added: 24-hour hours, an ER marker on the map, and emergency FAQs.
**Added:** neurology, cancer care, heart, surgery, maternity, pediatrics and lab services.

## 9. People in the photos, emergency first
**Found:** some service photos had no people, emergency care was a small tile, and Specialty care duplicated the specialty tiles.
**Changed:** used photos of people receiving care, made Emergency care a full-width featured card with an ambulance photo, and moved the specialties to their own page.

## 10. Doctor photos and a clean hospital photo
**Added:** portraits for the doctors, a Find a doctor directory with search and an "Accepting new patients" filter, and a clean Unsplash photo of the hospital without a baked-in logo.

## 11. Project notes
**Added:** a brand and design choices section explaining the magnolia, the palette and the photo, plus the designer's name and course.

## 12. Easier access to the sources and notes
**Found:** the only link to the project notes and sources was at the very bottom of the landing page, and the sources page itself was very long.
**Changed:** added a "Sources and notes" link to the top bar and phone menu, plus a "Sources and notes" button pinned to the bottom-left corner of every page. The sources page now uses collapsible sections with counts, "Expand all" and "Collapse all" buttons, citations behind toggles, and a "Back to top" button, which cut its length from about 14,000px to about 3,000px.

## 13. A planned, Google-style campus map
**Found:** the first embedded-style map had randomly placed buildings, and a later illustrated version did not look like the familiar web map visitors expect. The outpatient clinics were also not clearly shown.
**Changed:** rebuilt the map in a Google Maps style with a planned layout: a regular street grid with houses in tidy rows, a pink hospital campus with the Main Hospital, a clearly labeled Outpatient Center (linked by a covered walkway, with its own entrance) and the Emergency Room, plus a red main-entrance pin, an ER pin, blue parking and bus icons, the Magnolia Garden and nearby landmarks. The address card, directions (now including the Outpatient Center), zoom, recenter, drag and keyboard controls came back.

## 14. Campus layout fixes
**Found:** the Magnolia Garden sat right beside the Emergency Room, and the ER was a separate building away from the main hospital.
**Changed:** attached the Emergency Room to the main hospital's southeast corner, as in most real hospitals, and moved the garden to the west side beside visitor parking, away from the ER.

## 15. A properly planned campus layout
**Found:** the campus still read as buildings placed wherever they fit. The Outpatient Center's entrance faced a side street with no parking, visitor parking was far from the main entrance, and the address card covered the top of the campus.
**Changed:** replanned the campus as a zoned site plan around a north-south axis from Magnolia Grove Way to the main entrance, the way real hospitals separate their traffic:
- **Center:** the main hospital, with the main drive, drop-off loop, visitor parking garage and accessible parking all at the front door.
- **West:** the Outpatient Center, with its own entrance and parking lot entered from Laurel Ave, linked to the hospital by a covered walkway.
- **East:** the Emergency department, attached to the hospital, with ER parking and a separate ambulance-only drive off Birch St, so emergency traffic never crosses visitor traffic.
- **North:** the Magnolia Garden in the quiet northwest corner, away from the ER, and a delivery yard off Elm St at the back.
The map now opens with the whole campus clear of the address card. The directions, the parking note and the screen-reader description all match the new layout.

**Follow-up:** on smaller laptop screens the address card still covered the garden, so the map now measures the card when it loads and starts the campus just below it, while keeping Magnolia Grove Way in view.

## 16. Search finds doctors too
**Found:** the header search only filtered the services list, so typing "doctors" or a doctor's name did nothing useful.
**Changed:** the header search now says "Search services or doctors". Searches for doctors ("doctors", "heart doctor", "Dr. Reyes", "Spanish doctor") open the Find a doctor page already filtered, and service searches like "MRI" still filter the services on the landing page.

## 17. Trust badges and a real patient portal page
**Found:** a formal design review of a separate Canva mockup of the landing page flagged missing accreditation signals, and the "Patient portal" link only opened a "not part of this concept site yet" message, even though the case study's "what I would do next" section called for a patient portal mockup.
**Changed:** added a "Recognized for quality care" row to the About section (Joint Commission accredited, Magnet recognized nursing, "A" safety grade from Leapfrog, Top 100 rural and community hospital, labeled as sample recognitions), and built a fifth page, `patient-portal.html`, in the established brand style. Every "Patient portal" link and button across the site (utility bar, quick actions, mobile menu, footer) now points to it instead of opening a placeholder message.
**Patient portal page:** a tabbed Home / Appointments / Messages & records layout, previewable as three sample patients (an adult managing their own care, a parent managing a child's care, and a teen whose guardian still shares some records) to show how the design adapts to different patients. Appointments link out to the matching doctor on the Find a doctor page. Clearly labeled as a sample preview with no real account or data connected.

## 18. A design-master pass on the patient portal, richer care cards, and a design review
**Found:** the patient portal page worked but looked plain next to the rest of the site: plain-bordered cards instead of the signature green card fill, no persistent sense of whose record you were looking at, and no motion to match the landing page's gentle scroll fade-in. Separately, the Primary care and Diagnostic imaging cards on the landing page only had a one-line description each, thinner than Emergency care's fuller copy.
**Changed:** rebuilt the patient portal around a two-column layout: a sticky "patient rail" on the left with an avatar, the sample patient's name, their care-team link and an access-scope note, next to the tabbed Home / Appointments / Messages & records content on the right. Stat cards and appointment cards now use the site's green card-fill hover and focus effect, the hero has the same magnolia watermark and gentle scroll fade-in as the landing page, and persona-switcher buttons show avatar initials. Added a short checklist of what is covered to the Primary care and Diagnostic imaging cards on the landing page (annual physicals and chronic conditions for primary care; X-ray, ultrasound, CT and MRI for imaging), plus a matching row of specialty chips on the Specialty care card so the three cards read as one consistent set.
**Design review found and fixed:** on a phone-width screen, the patient portal's rail card and tab bar ran past the right edge of the screen, because the two-column layout did not set a safe minimum width for its columns. Fixed by constraining both columns so they always shrink to fit, and added a soft fade to the edge of the tab bar as a cue that it scrolls sideways when the tab labels do not all fit.


## 19. Primary care and Diagnostic imaging get their own pages
**Found:** the Specialty care card on the landing page links out to a full page of specialty types, but the Primary care and Diagnostic imaging cards only opened a short pop-up message. That made the three "Find care" cards inconsistent with each other.
**Changed:** built two new pages in the same style as the Specialty care page: `primary-care.html` (annual physicals and wellness visits, vaccinations and preventive screenings, chronic conditions, and referrals and care coordination) and `imaging.html` (X-ray and fluoroscopy, ultrasound, CT scan, and MRI and mammography), each with its own photo, a "what to expect" or "what a visit usually covers" list, and a getting-started note. The Primary care and Diagnostic imaging cards on the landing page now link to these pages the same way the Specialty care card links to `specialties.html`, and the old pop-up messages for both were removed. Every page's footer and the landing page's footer navigation now link to both new pages. New photos for both pages came from Unsplash and are credited on the sources and notes page.

## 20. Patient portal becomes a dashboard, and "Patient portal" moves into the main nav bar
**Found:** "Patient portal" only appeared in the desktop-only utility bar at the very top and at the end of the mobile menu, not in the main nav row shown on every screen size. Separately, a reference design the designer liked used a sidebar with seven sections (Dashboard, Appointments, Secure messages, Medications, Test results, Billing & insurance, Find care & support) instead of the portal's three-tab Home / Appointments / Messages & records layout.
**Changed:** added a "Patient portal" link near the top of both the desktop primary nav row and the mobile menu (right after Home), alongside its existing utility-bar and footer links. Rebuilt `patient-portal.html` to closely follow the reference's own layout rather than translating it loosely into the site's existing patterns: a bordered portal frame holding a cream sidebar next to a white content area, with a seven-item nav list (Dashboard, Appointments, Secure messages, Medications, Test results, Billing & insurance, Find care & support) that scrolls the single always-visible dashboard to the matching card instead of swapping between separate tab panels. Persona pill-tabs are ordered Parents & caregivers, Teens, Adults, matching the reference exactly, and a working Desktop/Mobile toggle swaps the frame for an actual phone-style mockup with its own bottom icon bar, both driving the same underlying content. Cards are simple and bordered with a small icon badge, matching the reference's plainer card style rather than the site's green fill-card hover. The old "Messages & records" tab's lab results and prescriptions moved into dedicated Medications and Test results cards; new Billing & insurance and Find care & support cards were added, the latter linking out to Primary care, Specialty care, Diagnostic imaging and Find a doctor. The teen persona's billing card stays guardian-only, consistent with its existing "shared until 18" rule, and its Test results card links a sample MRI result straight to the Diagnostic imaging page. The already-established named sample patients (Morgan Ellis, Danielle Price and Noah, Jordan Reyes) were kept so the doctor cross-links and rest of the site stayed consistent.
**Design review found and fixed:** switching tabs or personas quickly, before the scroll-reveal animation had run, could leave the dashboard's stat cards and buttons permanently invisible, because the reveal only triggers once a section is visible on screen and a hidden tab panel never counts as visible. Fixed by removing the scroll-reveal effect from content inside the persona panels (it stays on the hero and persona switcher, which are never hidden), so every card now shows reliably no matter how quickly someone switches between personas or devices.
**First pass missed the mark:** an initial rebuild kept the seven-section idea but used the site's own tabbed-panel interaction, dropped the reference's device toggle, and kept the site's heavier card styling, on the reasoning that a toggle and demo sign-in buttons would be redundant on an already-real coded page. Direct feedback ("it doesn't look the same at all") made clear the ask was to match the reference's actual look and structure, not just borrow its section list. The rebuild above replaces that first pass.

## 21. Consistent colors, and a landing-page card fixed to match its siblings
**Found:** the Specialty care card on the landing page used a row of colored pill chips while Primary care and Diagnostic imaging used a checklist, so the three "Find care" cards no longer read as one set. Separately, the patient portal's card background colors were assigned per persona rather than per card type: Medications was pink on the parent dashboard but plain on the teen and adult ones, while Billing was plain for parent and teen but pink for adult, with no consistent meaning.
**Changed:** gave the Specialty care card the same four-item checklist format as the other two cards. Reassigned the portal's card colors so each card type has one fixed color everywhere, regardless of persona: Secure messages, Medications, Find care & support, and the persona-specific notice cards (Reminders, Guardian oversight) each keep the same background on every dashboard, while Appointments, Test results and Billing & insurance stay plain. Medications started out pink, which read as an alert color, so it was moved to green and the persona-specific notice cards took the pink instead.

## 22. The device toggle comes out; the portal is just responsive now
**Found:** after seeing the reference-matched rebuild, the designer asked for the Desktop/Mobile toggle and phone-style mockup to come out entirely. The portal should behave like every other page on the site: one layout that reflows for the screen it's on, not a simulated device preview to switch between.
**Changed:** removed the toggle buttons, the phone-frame mock-up, and the JavaScript that rebuilt a separate mobile copy of the dashboard. The portal frame's sidebar and seven-item nav now sit above the content and wrap into pill buttons below 800px, the same jump-to-section pattern `specialties.html` uses, while the card grid stacks to one column. The result is a single page that works at every width without a device switch, matching how the rest of the site already behaves on a phone.

## 23. Midterm feedback: a sitewide nav bar, and a sign-in step for the portal
**Found:** midterm feedback from professors flagged two gaps. First, only the landing page carried the full site navigation; Primary care, Specialty care, Diagnostic imaging, Find a doctor, Sources and notes, and the Patient portal each had just a logo and a single back link, so there was no consistent way to jump between pages once a visitor left the landing page. Second, the patient portal dropped a visitor straight into the dashboard with no sign-in step, and moving between the dashboard and a detail view relied on the sidebar alone, with no back button inside the detail views themselves.
**Changed:** added a shared site navigation row (Home, Primary care, Specialty care, Diagnostic imaging, Find a doctor, Patient portal, Sources & notes) to the sticky header of all six of those pages, with the current page marked so it is easy to tell where you are. For the portal, added a sample sign-in screen ahead of the dashboard, a short card with pre-filled demo credentials and a plain note that it is not connected to a real account, which gates the dashboard until submitted and then hands focus to the dashboard heading. Added a Sign out button next to the sample-patient chip that returns to the sign-in screen, and added a "Back to dashboard" button at the top of all eighteen detail views (six views across three personas), reusing the page's existing view-switching logic so no new navigation system was needed.

## 24. The landing page gets the same nav row, and the portal's sticky header stops hiding content
**Found:** a closer look at Round 23 showed it had covered the six inner pages but skipped the landing page itself, so the one page most visitors start on was still the odd one out, with an anchor-only nav that could not reach Primary care, Specialty care, Diagnostic imaging or Sources & notes directly. Separately, the portal's sign-in and sign-out transitions scroll the page to the next section, but nothing accounted for the sticky header's height, so right after signing in the persona switcher and part of the "Explore the portal" heading landed hidden underneath the header, and signing out did the same thing to the sign-in heading.
**Changed:** added the same seven-link site navigation row (Home, Primary care, Specialty care, Diagnostic imaging, Find a doctor, Patient portal, Sources & notes) to `index.html` and `standalone.html`, right under the landing page's existing header, so all seven pages now carry identical navigation and the landing page's own section links (Find care, About, Plan your visit, Billing, Contact) stay exactly as they were. For the portal, replaced the plain scroll-into-view calls with one that measures the sticky header's actual height first and scrolls just clear of it, used by sign-in, sign-out, and the "Skip to the portal preview" link alike (which now also opens the sign-in form instead of a hidden section if no one has signed in yet). The header's height changes by viewport width since its nav wraps on narrow screens, so this is measured live rather than hard-coded.

## 25. Doctors and care pages point to each other, and request buttons carry context forward
**Found:** the Find a doctor page and the three care pages (Primary care, Specialty care, Diagnostic imaging) mostly linked only one way. Doctor cards on `doctors.html` already linked out to their specialty page, but Dr. Okafor's link pointed at a dead anchor on the old landing page instead of the new `primary-care.html`. Diagnostic imaging had no doctor at all to link to or from, so a visitor reading about X-rays, CT or MRI had no "meet the radiologist" path the way primary care and specialty visitors did. Separately, every "Request an appointment" and "Ask about a scan" button sitewide sent visitors to the same empty contact form, with no sign of which doctor or service they had started from, so the context from the page they were just reading was lost.
**Changed:** fixed Dr. Okafor's "About primary care" link to point to `primary-care.html`. Added a tenth doctor, Dr. Hana Kim (diagnostic radiology, fluent in English and Korean, a sample Unsplash portrait credited on the sources page), to `doctors.html`, and linked her from the X-ray, CT and MRI cards on `imaging.html` with the same "Meet Dr. X" pattern the other care pages already use; Ultrasound keeps its existing maternity-care link instead, since that is a better match. Every request button across `doctors.html`, `primary-care.html`, `specialties.html` and `imaging.html` (29 buttons in all) now carries the specific doctor's name or the specific service into the landing page's contact form, so the message field arrives pre-filled ("I'd like to request an appointment with Dr. Hana Kim," "I'd like to ask about CT scan") instead of empty. The handful of general "not sure who to see" links in each page's intro note were left generic on purpose, since they are not tied to one doctor or service.

## 26. An "Explore care" button on Find a doctor, and the patient portal drops its "sample" labeling
**Found:** two more rounds of feedback. First, `doctors.html` had no quick path back to the three care-type pages beyond the nav bar; browsing by doctor and browsing by care type were two separate starting points with no button connecting them. Second, the patient portal read like a prototype rather than a real one: a persistent banner under the header read "Concept prototype preview, not connected to real medical records," the sign-in card was labeled "Sample sign-in," and nearly every card on every dashboard (medications, test results, billing, messages) ended with a parenthetical like "(Sample entry.)" or "Sample records only."
**Changed:** added an "Explore care options" button to the `doctors.html` hero, linking to the landing page's Find care section so a visitor browsing doctors can jump straight to the three care-type overviews. For the portal, removed the persistent "concept prototype" banner, reworded the sign-in card and intro copy to read like a real sign-in rather than a labeled demo, and removed the repeated "sample," "demo" and "preview" qualifiers from the caregiver, teen and adult dashboards (card copy, the persona eyebrows, and the signed-in status line), so the content reads as real patient data rather than flagged placeholder text throughout. The one still-honest, low-key disclosure that this is a class project stays where it already lived, in the site footer and on the Sources &amp; notes page, rather than repeated across the portal.

## 27. More "sample" labeling comes out sitewide, Dr. Kim's photo gets fixed, two more imaging doctors join, and the landing page nav gets decluttered
**Found:** four more issues after a closer look. First, the "sample" labeling fix from Round 26 had only covered the patient portal; the landing page, the doctor directory and the three care pages still had their own "Sample profiles," "Sample story," "Sample events," "Sample list" and similar tags and pills scattered through them. Second, the stock photo picked for Dr. Hana Kim was sourced from a search that returned an unrelated photo literally titled "Man in white dress shirt holding black pen," so the portrait on her card showed a man under a woman's name. Third, diagnostic imaging had only one doctor (Dr. Kim) covering every scan type, with no one else to meet. Fourth, the landing page's header had grown to four stacked navigation rows: the utility bar, the logo and search row, the existing primary nav (mostly same-page section links), and the cross-page nav row added in Round 24, and the last two repeated several of the same links (Patient portal, Find a doctor, Plan your visit each appeared two or three times), reading as cluttered.
**Changed:** removed every remaining "sample," "demo" and "concept site" pill, tag and meta description across `standalone.html`, `index.html`, `doctors.html`, `primary-care.html`, `specialties.html` and `imaging.html` (the doctor and care-page hero pills, the clinicians/story/events/visit/coverage tags on the landing page, and the careers and privacy quick-answer copy), keeping the one honest disclosure where it already lived, in the footer and on Sources &amp; notes. Replaced Dr. Kim's photo with a correctly matched portrait and updated her credit on the sources page. Added two new imaging doctors, Dr. Marcus Webb (interventional radiology) and Dr. Naomi Clarke (mammography and breast imaging), each with their own photo, credit and doctor card; redistributed the "Meet the doctor" links on `imaging.html` so X-ray and fluoroscopy points to Dr. Webb, CT scan stays with Dr. Kim, and MRI and mammography points to Dr. Clarke, instead of all three cards pointing to the same person. Trimmed the landing page's cross-page nav row from seven links down to the four that weren't already reachable elsewhere on that page (Primary care, Specialty care, Diagnostic imaging, Sources &amp; notes), cutting the repeated Home, Patient portal and Find a doctor entries rather than removing the row outright.

## 28. A real "Find care" dropdown, Sources & notes moves into the main nav, and verification switches to actual screenshots
**Found:** the Round 27 nav trim was not enough. The landing page's header still carried two stacked navigation rows, the main one (Home, Patient portal, Find care, Doctors, About Magnolia, Plan your visit, Billing, Contact) and the leftover cross-page row underneath it (Primary care, Specialty care, Diagnostic imaging, Sources &amp; notes), so it still read as cluttered, and "Find care" led to a section that dumped all four care types (Emergency, Primary, Specialty, Diagnostic imaging) onto the page at once rather than letting a visitor choose one from the nav. Separately, verification up to this point had relied on reading the code rather than looking at the rendered page, which is how the duplicate nav row and Dr. Kim's wrong photo made it through earlier rounds without being caught sooner. This round, the sandbox's block on the Tailwind CDN (confirmed again by a direct request to it) was worked around by installing the Tailwind CLI from the npm registry, which is reachable even though the CDN is not, and building a real compiled stylesheet to screenshot a true rendered copy of the page with Playwright, including scrolling and clicking through it rather than just reading the HTML.
**Changed:** replaced the "Find care" link in both the desktop and mobile navigation with a real dropdown menu listing Emergency care, Primary care, Specialty care and Diagnostic imaging, each going straight to its own page (or to the Find care section for Emergency); removed the now-redundant cross-page nav row entirely, so the header is back to one navigation line. Moved "Sources &amp; notes" out of that removed row and into the last spot of the main nav, after Contact, on both the desktop and mobile menus. On the landing page itself, the Emergency care card stays visible by default since it is safety information, but the Primary care, Specialty care and Diagnostic imaging cards are now collapsed behind a "Browse all care types" button and no longer show automatically; typing in the services search box still works and now opens the collapsed cards automatically to show matches. Verified this round by actually rendering the page (dropdown open and closed, mobile submenu open, the care types collapsed and expanded, and a live search for "mri") rather than only checking the markup.

## 29. A "Doctors" dropdown, physician credentials, and a product/UX audit for professional polish
**Found:** a design audit against the product-design and ui-ux-design skills (an anti-slop scan plus a manual pass over the doctor directory, the patient portal sign-in, and the primary care page) turned up one gap serious enough to fix immediately: the doctor directory had no credentials under any of the twelve names anywhere, which is the single most "this is not a real doctor" tell a hospital site can have. The patient portal's sign-in card also had no "Forgot password?" affordance, something every real patient portal has even when, as here, the account is not real. Everything else checked out: the anti-slop scan passed clean on every page (the two things it flagged, an intentional focus-ring replacement and the "&larr;" back-arrow glyph, are both fine as built), and the primary care, doctor directory and portal sign-in pages already read as genuinely professional once rendered and looked at directly. Separately, the "Doctors" link in the main nav only scrolled to the three-doctor teaser section on the landing page, with no way to jump straight to a specialty from the nav the way the new "Find care" dropdown already allows for care types.
**Changed:** added a credentials line under every doctor's name on `doctors.html` ("MD &middot; Board certified in [specialty]", with the two newer radiologists noting their subspecialty), matching how real hospital directories present physicians. Added a "Forgot password?" toggle next to the password field on the patient portal sign-in card that reveals a short, honest note (this demo does not send real reset emails, and the pre-filled credentials are there to look around) rather than leaving the field with no recovery option at all. Turned "Doctors" in the main nav into a second dropdown, alongside "Find care," listing all twelve doctors grouped into ten specialties (Primary care, Emergency medicine, Cardiology, Neurology, Oncology, General surgery, Obstetrics &amp; gynecology, Pediatrics, Rehabilitation medicine, Diagnostic imaging) plus an "All doctors" link at the top, each specialty jumping straight to `doctors.html` pre-filtered to that search term; built as a two-column menu on desktop and a nested list on mobile, reusing and generalizing the dropdown code from the Find care menu so only one dropdown can be open at a time and both close the same way (click outside, Escape, or picking an item). Verified by rendering and clicking through the new dropdown (desktop and mobile), confirming each specialty link lands on the right filtered doctor, and checking the credentials line and the forgot-password note.

## 30. The submitted zip wouldn't open, and a second ui-ux-design pass catches a nav-wrap bug the eye missed
**Found:** the designer reported the submission zip was broken. Testing the exact file that had been delivered with three independent tools (the system's own unzip, the zip utility's self-test, and Python's zipfile module) found no corruption in the archive itself, so the most likely cause was the file getting interrupted or cached stale somewhere between being built and being downloaded, not a flaw in how it was built. Separately, asked to double-check everything with the ui-ux-design skill, a second pass ran the skill's two gating scripts (an anti-slop detector and a motion checker) across all eight pages plus a manual keyboard-navigation and color-contrast check on everything added in Rounds 28 and 29. The motion checker flagged standalone.html and index.html for animating `width` in a keyframe, a real performance problem if true (it forces the browser to redo layout on every frame instead of just compositing), but tracing it back showed the checker's simple pattern-matching had run past the end of the actual animation (which only moves `opacity` and `translate`, exactly as it should) and mistakenly blamed an unrelated, non-animated `width` rule many lines later; nothing needed to change there. The real find was in the primary nav: adding "Sources &amp; notes" as a ninth item in Round 28 pushed the row just over its available width at the narrow end of desktop sizes (roughly 1024 to 1080 pixels wide), so two or three nav labels wrapped onto a second line there, something none of the earlier screenshots happened to be taken at exactly that width.
**Changed:** rebuilt the submission zip from scratch with stricter flags and verified the new one three independent ways (unzip's test mode, zip's own self-test, and Python's zipfile module all report it clean), and separately confirmed every HTML file inside it still passes the tag-balance and duplicate-ID checks. Narrowed the primary nav's link spacing from a 2rem gap to 1.25rem, which keeps all nine items on one line from the 1024-pixel breakpoint up through wide desktop screens, confirmed by measuring the nav row's rendered height at several widths in that range before and after. Confirmed with a scripted keyboard pass that both dropdown menus open on Enter, move focus into the first item on Tab, close and return focus to their trigger on Escape, and that opening one dropdown closes the other, and confirmed the color contrast of everything added the last two rounds (the forgot-password link and note, the dropdown menu links, and the doctor credentials line) all comfortably clear the AA text-contrast minimum.

## 31. The back buttons go, two files become one, and the patient portal gets closer to a real one
**Found:** the designer said the back buttons were disliked, with no further detail, so the six sub-pages (`doctors.html`, `primary-care.html`, `specialties.html`, `imaging.html`, `patient-portal.html`, `sources.html`) were screenshotted as actually rendered rather than read as code. Every one of them turned out to carry a pill-shaped "Back to the site" or "All care options" button in the top right of the sticky header, sitting directly above a full seven-item primary nav that already repeats Home and the current page's own highlighted entry, on top of a brand logo that already links home. The button was pure duplicate navigation with inconsistent wording page to page, not a one-off visual flaw. Separately, the designer asked to get rid of `standalone.html`; the project had carried two near-identical landing pages since early on, `index.html` (written for pasting into Canva, with `canva://` image links) and `standalone.html` (the same page with local image paths, meant to be opened outside Canva), kept in sync by hand every round, which `tech-notes.md` had already flagged as worth combining once the Canva link stopped mattering.
